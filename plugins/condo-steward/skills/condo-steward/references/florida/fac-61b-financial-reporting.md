# Florida Administrative Code 61B — Division rules on budgets, reserves and financial reporting

**Currency:** rules as published through mid-2026 (61B-23.002 on board-meeting notice was revised effective June 2026). The Division revises 61B periodically to follow statute changes. These rules bind condominiums (Ch. 718) and, in parallel form, cooperatives (Ch. 719). HOAs (Ch. 720) have no equivalent rulemaking body.

## 61B-22.003 — Budgets

- The annual budget must show **each line item** of estimated revenue and expense, in enough detail for owners to understand it, and must separately state **reserve accounts** for each required component.
- Reserve line items must disclose, for each component: estimated remaining useful life, estimated replacement (or deferred maintenance) cost, estimated current fund balance, and the resulting annual funding requirement.
- If the association pools reserves, the budget shows the pooled schedule instead.
- Budgets must be kept as official records and available to owners.

## 61B-22.005 — Reserves

- **Straight-line (component) method**: for each item, `annual contribution = (estimated replacement cost − current fund balance) ÷ estimated remaining useful life`. Interest earned stays with the component (or is allocated).
- **Pooled (cash-flow) method**: multiple components are funded from one pool sized so the pool never drops below zero across the projection horizon (the *baseline* funding goal), or higher if the board chooses. Switching between pooled and straight-line presentation mid-stream has procedural consequences — read 61B-22.005 and ask the CPA before changing method.
- Reserve funds and their accrued interest are **restricted** to the purposes for which they were established. Using them for another purpose (including operating shortfalls) requires the owner vote described in 718.112(2)(f) — and for SIRS items is no longer permitted at all.
- Waiver or reduction of reserves must be approved at a duly noticed meeting by **a majority of the total voting interests** (not just those present), with the statutory disclosure language on the ballot and in the budget. The vote is effective for **one budget year only**.
- The Division's rule also governs the wording and placement of the bold "reserve waiver" warnings on budgets and financial reports.

## 61B-22.006 — Year-end financial reporting

The year-end financial statements (compiled, reviewed, or audited under 718.111(13)) must:

- be prepared on the **fund basis** (at minimum: operating and reserve funds shown separately, or a note disclosing the same);
- disclose, for each reserve component or pool: **beginning balance, additions, expenditures, ending balance** and the method used (straight-line or pooled);
- disclose whether reserves were waived or reduced, and the amount of any reserves actually funded compared with the amount required;
- disclose special assessments (amount, purpose, and whether used for that purpose);
- disclose related-party transactions, guarantees, and the manner in which reserve funds are invested or commingled;
- include a statement of revenues and expenses compared to the budget, if the report is compiled or a cash receipts report.

The **cash receipts and expenditures report** (revenues under $150,000) must show receipts and expenditures by classification consistent with the budget, and separate reserve activity.

## 61B-23.002 — Board meetings and notices (financial items)

- Budget and special assessment meetings: 14-day mailed/delivered notice plus posted notice; notice must state the purpose and estimated cost of special assessments.
- Records of the vote to waive reserves are official records and must show the number of voting interests approving.

## Checklist: reading a year-end report against these rules

1. Operating and reserve funds shown separately? If not, ask the CPA.
2. Each reserve component's roll-forward (beginning + contributions + interest − spending = ending) present and matching the balance sheet?
3. Reserve contributions **funded** (cash actually moved) vs. **accrued** (booked but still sitting in operating cash)? A gap shows up as a due-to/due-from between funds.
4. Waiver language present if reserves were reduced?
5. Special assessments disclosed with use of proceeds?
6. Any related-party or manager-related disclosures?
