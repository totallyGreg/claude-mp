# Florida Chapter 718 — The Condominium Act (working summary)

**Currency:** reflects Chapter 718 as amended through the 2025 regular session (notably SB 4-D 2022, SB 154 2023, HB 1021 2024, HB 913 2025); spot-checked against Online Sunshine in September 2026. The Legislature amends this chapter almost every year. Before anyone acts on a section below, read the current text and, for anything with money or liability attached, involve the association's attorney.

## Official records — 718.111(12)

- The association must keep a defined list of records (declaration, bylaws, rules, minutes, insurance policies, contracts, accounting records, budgets, financial reports, election materials, inspection reports, etc.). Most must be kept **7 years**; some (declaration, minutes, plans) permanently.
- Records must be available for inspection within **10 working days** of a written request. Failure creates a rebuttable presumption of willful violation and statutory damages; repeated or knowing violations can carry criminal penalties for board members and managers (2024 amendments).
- Associations with **25 or more units** must post specified records on a password-protected website or app (threshold lowered from 150 units by the 2024 amendments); new records must be posted within 30 days of creation. Verify the current document list against 718.111(12)(g).
- Certain records are **not** accessible to owners: attorney-client privileged material, personnel records, medical records, owner email addresses/phone numbers (unless the owner consented), security information.

## Financial reporting — 718.111(13)

Within 90 days after fiscal year end (or as the bylaws provide), the association must prepare or obtain a year-end financial report. The required level depends on **total annual revenues**:

| Total annual revenues | Report |
|---|---|
| under $150,000 | report of cash receipts and expenditures |
| $150,000 – under $300,000 | compiled financial statements (CPA) |
| $300,000 – under $500,000 | reviewed financial statements (CPA) |
| $500,000 or more | audited financial statements (CPA) |

- Owners may vote to require a *lesser* report (or none), but only by a **majority of all voting interests**, **before** the fiscal year ends, and the waiver **may not be used for consecutive fiscal years** — 718.111(13)(d).
- The report (or notice that it is available) must go to owners within **21 days after completion and no later than 180 days after fiscal year end** — 718.111(13)(a).
- Monthly manager statements are *unaudited*; they are not the statutory report. See `../financial-accounting.md`.

## Budget adoption — 718.112(2)(e)

- The board adopts an annual budget of estimated revenues and expenses. The budget meeting needs **14 days'** mailed/delivered/electronic notice **and** posted notice, with a copy of the proposed budget.
- If the adopted budget exceeds **115%** of the prior year's assessments (excluding reserves and non-recurring items), **10%** of the voting interests can petition for a special meeting to consider an alternative budget.

## Reserves — 718.112(2)(f)

- The budget **must include reserves** for capital expenditures and deferred maintenance: roof replacement, building painting, pavement resurfacing, and any item whose deferred maintenance or replacement cost exceeds **$25,000 or the Division's inflation-adjusted amount, whichever is greater** (raised from $10,000 by the 2025 amendments). Reserves are computed from estimated remaining useful life and replacement cost; components may be **pooled** (cash-flow method) under 61B-22.005.
- Reserve funds may be used only for their designated purpose unless the owners approve otherwise by majority of total voting interests (with statutory exceptions). Since 2024–2025 amendments, the ability to waive or reduce reserves, and to use reserve funds for other purposes, is **prohibited for SIRS items** in buildings three or more stories tall (budgets adopted on/after Dec 31, 2024). Non-SIRS items may still be waived by majority of total voting interests.
- 2025 (HB 913) added flexibility: after a milestone inspection, an association may by owner vote pause or reduce reserve contributions for up to two consecutive annual budgets to fund identified repairs; associations may fund reserves through loans or lines of credit with owner approval; and certain reserve investments are authorized. **Verify the exact conditions before using any of these.**
- Budget disclosure: if reserves were waived or reduced, the budget and the financial report must carry the statutory bold-type warning to owners.

## Board meetings — 718.112(2)(c)

- Notice posted conspicuously **48 continuous hours** in advance (except emergencies). Owners may attend and speak on agenda items.
- **14-day** mailed and posted notice when the board will consider non-emergency special assessments, amendments to rules regarding unit use, or the budget. Special assessment notices must state the purpose and estimated cost.
- Since 2024, boards of associations with **10 or more units** must meet at least once each quarter, and at least four times a year the agenda must let owners ask questions about inspections, structural integrity, and finances. Division rule 61B-23.002 (revised 2026) governs the mechanics of notice.
- Board members may participate by telephone or video; a quorum may be established that way. Minutes are official records.
- Meetings of committees that spend money or make recommendations to the board on the budget are also open.

## Elections and board service — 718.112(2)(d)

- Annual meeting each year; first notice of election at least **60 days** before; candidates give notice **40 days** before; second notice with ballot **14–34 days** before. No quorum is needed to elect, but **20%** of eligible voters must cast ballots.
- Board members must, within **90 days** of election or appointment, **both** sign the written certification that they have read the governing documents **and** complete a DBPR-approved educational course of at least 4 hours (2024 amendment), plus at least 1 hour of continuing education each year. Certificates are official records; a director who does not comply is suspended from the board until they do.
- Term limit: no more than **8 consecutive years** unless approved by **two-thirds of all votes cast in the election** or there are not enough candidates.
- Directors who are 90+ days delinquent are deemed to have abandoned their seat; directors charged with certain crimes are removed.

## Assessments, liens, collections — 718.116

- Owners are liable for assessments from the date of ownership; a new owner is jointly liable with the previous owner for unpaid amounts (lender "safe harbor" limits apply to first-mortgagee foreclosures).
- Interest at the rate in the declaration (default/maximum **18%**), and a late fee up to the greater of **$25 or 5%** of the installment if the declaration or bylaws allow.
- Before recording a **claim of lien**, the association must send **45 days'** written notice of intent to lien; before filing a foreclosure, another **45 days'** notice of intent to foreclose. Notices have statutory content and delivery requirements.
- The association may demand rent directly from a delinquent owner's tenant.
- Payments are applied first to interest, then late fees, then attorney's fees/costs, then the assessment — the statute overrides any owner's "restrictive endorsement."
- Owners more than 90 days delinquent can have common-element and amenity use rights suspended (718.303(4)); **voting** rights may be suspended only when the delinquency exceeds **$1,000** and 90 days (718.303(5)).

**Ask the attorney before:** recording a lien, foreclosing, entering into a payment plan that waives statutory rights, or applying suspensions.

## Fines — 718.303

- Up to **$100 per violation per day**, maximum **$1,000** in aggregate for a continuing violation. Fines cannot become a lien on a condominium unit.
- Requires **14 days'** written notice and an opportunity for a hearing before a committee of owners who are not board members or their relatives. The committee's vote is required for the fine to stand.

## Contracts and conflicts — 718.3026, 718.3027

- Contracts for products or services exceeding **5% of the total annual budget (including reserves)** require competitive bids (not necessarily the lowest). Exempt: attorneys, accountants, architects, engineers, landscape architects, community association managers, and employees.
- Directors and officers must disclose conflicts; contracts with a director or a director's relative or company need a specific approval process and disclosure. Since 2024, additional prohibitions and criminal penalties cover kickbacks, voter fraud, and misuse of records.

## Insurance — 718.111(11)

- The association must carry adequate property insurance based on **replacement cost**, determined by an independent appraisal at least every **36 months**, plus the required windstorm and flood considerations.
- **Fidelity (crime) coverage** must cover the maximum funds in the custody of the association or its manager at any one time — check this against the cash balances on the monthly statement.

## Electronic voting — 718.128

Owners may vote online once the board adopts a resolution authorizing it (at a meeting noticed with the intent to consider it) and each owner consents in writing; the system must authenticate identity, keep votes secret where the vote is secret, and produce a record. Verify the current text before adopting a resolution — the 2024–2025 sessions adjusted consent and notice details.

## Disputes — 718.1255

Many owner–association disputes (records, elections, meetings, use restrictions) must go through DBPR mandatory nonbinding arbitration or presuit mediation before court. Assessment collection is generally excluded and goes to court.

## Reading this alongside a financial statement

- Cash and reserve figures on the monthly statement should be consistent with the **adopted budget's reserve schedule** and the **SIRS**.
- Delinquency figures determine whether **718.116** notices are due and whether suspension rights exist.
- Total annual revenue on the budget determines the **718.111(13)** report tier the association must obtain at year end.
- Fidelity coverage limits should exceed the largest cash total on the statement.
