# Florida Chapter 720 — Homeowners' Associations (working summary)

**Currency:** through the 2025 regular session (HB 1203 2024 and HB 913 2025 made significant changes). HOAs are **not** regulated by DBPR's Division of Condominiums except for election and recall arbitration; there is no 61B rule chapter for them.

**Linking convention:** `http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0720/Sections/0720.NNN.html` — e.g. [720.303](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0720/Sections/0720.303.html). Chapter index: <http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0720/0720ContentsIndex.html>.

Use this file only when the community is a homeowners' association (parcels/lots) rather than a condominium. Cooperatives are Chapter 719 and track Chapter 718 closely.

## Key differences from condominiums

- **Reserves are optional** unless the developer established them or a majority of the total voting interests voted to require them. Once "statutory" reserves exist, they must be funded and can only be waived by owner vote with the same style of disclosures as condominiums. Many HOAs carry only "non-statutory" board-elected reserves, which the board can adjust.
- No SIRS or milestone inspection requirement (unless the HOA contains a 3+ story residential building it is responsible for — rare).
- **Financial reporting tiers** mirror 718 ($150k / $300k / $500k revenue thresholds → cash receipts / compiled / reviewed / audited), due within **90 days** after fiscal year end, with copies or availability notice to members within **21 days** of completion. Members can vote for a lesser report (limits apply).
- **Records**: 10 business days to make records available; associations with **100 or more parcels** must post specified records on a website or app (since Jan 1, 2025). Knowing destruction or refusal to provide records carries criminal exposure for directors (2024).
- **Board education**: directors must complete an approved 4-hour course within 90 days of election and annual continuing education (2024).
- **Fines**: up to $100 per day, maximum $1,000 unless the governing documents provide otherwise; 14-day notice and a hearing before an independent committee; fines of $1,000+ **can** become a lien on the parcel (unlike condominiums). 2024 amendments prohibit fines for certain trivial matters and require the notice to specify the violation and cure.
- **Assessment liens**: 45-day notice of intent to lien and 45-day notice of intent to foreclose (720.3085); interest and late fees as in the declaration; payment application order is set by statute.
- **Board meetings**: 48-hour posted notice; 14-day notice for special assessments and rules on parcel use; owners may speak on agenda items.
- **Member meetings**: annual meeting with 14-day notice; elections per bylaws (no 60/40/14-day condo schedule unless bylaws say so).
- **Contracts**: competitive bids when a contract exceeds 10% of the total annual budget including reserves (professionals and managers exempt).

## Reading the financial statement

- Determine first whether reserves are statutory or board-elected — the answer changes whether a negative reserve balance is a legal problem or only a planning problem.
- Fines and collection costs appear in receivables; unlike condominiums, large unpaid fines may be liened.
- Total revenue drives the year-end report tier just as in 718.
