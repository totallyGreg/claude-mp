# Florida structural safety: Milestone Inspections and SIRS

**Currency:** post-Surfside framework created by SB 4-D (2022), revised by SB 154 (2023), HB 1021 (2024) and HB 913 (2025). Deadlines and thresholds have shifted in each session — **verify the current text** and check with the local building department.

**Official text:** milestone inspections [553.899](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0553/Sections/0553.899.html) · SIRS [718.112](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0718/Sections/0718.112.html) paragraph (2)(g) · reserves generally 718.112(2)(f). Cite one of these links in every answer.

Applies to **condominium and cooperative buildings three or more habitable stories** tall. Two-story and smaller buildings are outside both programs (but still have ordinary reserve duties under 718.112(2)(f)).

## Milestone inspection — § 553.899

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who | Buildings 3+ stories, condominium or cooperative form of ownership |
| When | By December 31 of the year the building reaches **30 years** from the certificate of occupancy, then every **10 years**. The local enforcement agency may set **25 years** based on local environmental conditions (e.g., proximity to salt water). |
| Who performs it | A Florida-licensed architect or engineer |
| Phase 1 | Visual examination of habitable and non-habitable areas, including load-bearing elements; if no substantial structural deterioration is found, done. |
| Phase 2 | If Phase 1 finds substantial structural deterioration: destructive or non-destructive testing as needed, with a report of recommended repairs and any unsafe conditions. |
| Notice | The local enforcement agency notifies the association; the association has **180 days** to complete Phase 1. |
| Reporting | Inspector sends a sealed report to the association and the local agency; the association must distribute a summary to owners within **45 days** (and post it with official records). |
| Deferral | Deferrals are limited and local-agency dependent; do not assume one is available. |

## Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) — § 718.112(2)(g)

- Required every **10 years** for buildings 3+ stories. Initial studies were due by **December 31, 2024**; 2025 legislation created limited extensions tied to milestone-inspection timing — verify whether one applies.
- Must be performed by a licensed engineer or architect, or a **reserve specialist / professional reserve analyst**, with the visual inspection portion performed by a licensed engineer or architect.
- Covers, at minimum: roof; structure including load-bearing walls and primary structural members and systems; fireproofing and fire-protection systems; plumbing; electrical systems; waterproofing and exterior painting; windows and exterior doors; and any other item with a deferred-maintenance or replacement cost over **$25,000 (or the Division's inflation-adjusted figure, whichever is greater)** whose failure would negatively affect the items above — raised from $10,000 in 2025.
- For each item: remaining useful life, estimated replacement/deferred-maintenance cost, and a **recommended annual reserve amount**. Pooling is permitted; a baseline (never below zero) approach is allowed for SIRS pooled reserves.
- **Reserve funding for SIRS items cannot be waived or reduced** for budgets adopted on or after Dec 31, 2024, and SIRS reserve funds may not be used for other purposes. This is the single biggest change for association budgets.
- 2025 (HB 913): after a milestone inspection, owners may vote to pause or reduce reserve contributions for up to two consecutive budgets to fund the identified repairs; loans and lines of credit may fund reserves; investment of reserve funds is permitted under stated conditions. Each has procedural requirements.
- The SIRS is an **official record** and must be provided to prospective purchasers; developers must complete one before turnover.

## What to check on the financial statement

1. Does the reserve schedule contain line items matching the SIRS components (roof, structure, plumbing, electrical, waterproofing/painting, windows/doors, fire protection)?
2. Are the **budgeted annual contributions** for those items at least what the SIRS recommends? Look at "Reserve Income" budget vs. actual year-to-date.
3. Are any reserve components **negative**? A negative SIRS component means money designated for structural items has been spent or borrowed against — ask the manager and CPA how it will be restored, and the attorney whether it was permissible.
4. Are milestone/SIRS **professional fees** and any **repair projects** identified in Phase 2 reflected in the budget or a special assessment plan?

## Who to involve

- **Engineer / architect**: milestone inspections, Phase 2 findings, repair scope.
- **Reserve specialist**: SIRS preparation and updates.
- **Attorney**: whether waivers, pauses, or loans are permitted and how to notice and vote them.
- **CPA**: how pooled/straight-line reserves are presented and restored in the financial statements.
