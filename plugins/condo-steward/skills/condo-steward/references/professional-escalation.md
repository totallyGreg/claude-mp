# When to say "ask a professional" — and how to say it

The skill's job is to make owners and board members informed clients, not to substitute for licensed advice. Give the substantive answer first, then the escalation — never the other way round, and never as a blanket disclaimer.

## Escalate when the answer would

- create or enforce a **legal right** against a person (lien, fine, suspension, foreclosure, eviction, records-request denial);
- involve a **vote** whose validity depends on procedure (reserve waiver, budget over 115%, borrowing, amendments, recalls);
- **restate or reclassify** financial statements (negative reserves, fund transfers, capitalization, tax elections);
- assess **structural safety** or **useful life** of a building component;
- interpret the community's **own documents** (declaration, bylaws) where they conflict with or are stricter than the statute — the skill has not read them unless the user provided them;
- bind the association to a **contract** or **loan**.

Do not escalate for: explaining what a statute or a statement line means, computing ratios, drafting agendas or questions, summarizing options, or pointing out inconsistencies. That is the job.

## Who does what

| Professional | Bring in for | Typical trigger phrases |
|---|---|---|
| **Association attorney** (Florida community-association practice) | Collections and liens; fines; elections and recalls; meeting/notice validity; amendments; contracts; records disputes; DBPR complaints; anything with "can we / must we" attached to a statute | "can we fine", "put a lien", "waive reserves", "special assessment", "records request", "recall" |
| **CPA** (community-association experience) | Year-end compilation/review/audit; tax return (1120-H vs 1120); reserve presentation; fund transfers; internal-control questions about the manager | "audit", "tax", "restate", "negative reserve", "due to/from", "what basis" |
| **Reserve specialist** (RS/PRA) | Reserve study; SIRS; funding plans; component lives | "reserve study", "percent funded", "SIRS", "how much should we put away" |
| **Licensed engineer / architect** | Milestone inspection; structural findings; scope of repairs; permits | "cracks", "spalling", "milestone", "phase 2", "roof condition" |
| **Insurance agent / appraiser** | Coverage adequacy; replacement-cost appraisal (every 36 months); fidelity bond limits; claims | "are we covered", "appraisal", "crime policy", "hurricane" |
| **Licensed community association manager (CAM)** | Day-to-day operations and statutory notices — but the CAM works for the board, and the board remains responsible | "the manager says" — verify, don't defer |
| **DBPR Division / Ombudsman** | Election disputes, records complaints, mandatory arbitration; free owner resources | "file a complaint", "ombudsman" |

## Always link the law

Every answer that states a rule cites the section **and** links the official text (Online Sunshine for statutes, flrules.org for 61B rules — URL patterns are at the top of each Florida reference). If the reference file's "as of" date predates the most recent legislative session, say so and offer to fetch the current text.

## Phrasing that works

- "Here is what §718.116 requires and what your numbers show. Because recording a lien creates a legal claim against the owner, have the association's attorney send the 45-day notice — most firms do this at a fixed fee."
- "The negative reserve component almost certainly reflects how the manager presents pooled reserves, but only the CPA who prepares the year-end report can confirm whether restricted funds were used. Ask for the reserve roll-forward."
- "Nothing in the statute prevents this, but your declaration may. Check Article X before the meeting, or ask counsel to."

## Phrasing to avoid

- Generic "consult a professional" with no indication of *which* one or *why*.
- Refusing to explain the law or the numbers because a professional exists.
- Presenting a rule-of-thumb ratio as a legal requirement.
- Guessing at the contents of governing documents the user has not shared.
